RBX Labs Research & Practice Lab

Study the work. Build the practice.

As AI moves from prompts to agents and operational systems, the Lab studies what is delegated, what evidence supports the result, what remains human-owned, and who is accountable. We turn those questions into research, workshops, prototypes, evaluations, and sponsored pilots through RBX Labs.

Two connected research lanes

One asks how people should work with capable AI. The other asks how AI systems should behave when the work becomes operational.

01 · AI judgment and delegated thinking

Delegate tasks without outsourcing judgment

Build clearer practices for deciding when to delegate, when to question, when to verify, when to reflect, and what must remain owned by a person or institution.

Current artifacts: WAILS research, AI Judgment Literacy, Delegated Thinking, SIGCSE curriculum, and practitioner workshops.

02 · Runtime trust and open-source AI

Make operational AI easier to evaluate and govern

Develop practical signals and controls for model, agent, tool, and component behavior: evaluation gates, permissions, monitoring, escalation, and replayable traces.

Current artifacts: FOSSY Runtime Trust Kit, public-safety agent evaluation, Network Guardian, and open-source AI trust work.

Open Source AI / OSI conversation

A research portfolio for open systems that remain accountable in operation.

RBX Labs connects open-source AI runtime trust with institutional governance and human agency. The work is offered as bounded research, workshop, and prototype material—not as an OSAID certification or a claim that openness makes a system safe by default.

01 · Open-source AI runtime

From open artifacts to runtime trust

The FOSSY research brief and Apache-2.0 Runtime Trust Kit examine how permissions, provenance, evaluation, downstream-state verification, and replayable traces make deployed AI systems more inspectable and repairable.

Contribution: a maintainer-oriented workshop around the loop failure → trace → fixture → evaluation → regression test → patch.

02 · Institutional governance

AI-assisted peer review decision trace

A governance proposal for keeping AI assistance evidence-linked, testable, contestable, and subordinate to human editorial authority.

Trace: manuscript claim → linked evidence → testable concern → human disposition → accountable decision.

Position paper and public-material demonstrator; not an autonomous referee.
03 · Human agency

From AI Use to AI Judgment

From AI Use to AI Judgment: A Framework for Responsible AI Literacy in Education is a conceptual and pedagogical position paper submitted to the WAILS 2026 workshop.

Framework: Decide → Question → Verify → Reflect → Own.

The paper and AI Judgment Lab prototype demonstrate an inspectable practice; they do not claim validated learning or transfer.
04 · Emerging measurement

Delegated Thinking

The NeurIPS research-in-development paper proposes matched task pairs and decision traces for studying capability-relevant delegation.

Shared as an emerging research direction, not as a finished submission or validated measure.

Ways to work with the Lab

The Lab is not a separate software product. It is a focused program for turning questions into credible, useful work.

01

Sponsored research pilot

A focused 4-8 week study with a defined question, partner context, evidence plan, and public or private output.

02

AI judgment workshop

A half-day or multi-week program that helps a team define useful delegation, verification, escalation, and ownership practices.

03

Applied evaluation partner

Independent evaluation, tutorial design, or practice review for AI products, agents, open-source components, and public-interest systems.
