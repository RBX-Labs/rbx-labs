The problem

Community health is hard to see when the tools are scattered.

WeKamp started from a practical problem: useful signals existed, but they were buried.

Creators and guides could feel issues before they could explain them. The team could see activity, but not always quality.

The real problem is not conversation. It is coordination, trust, and lifecycle management.

WeKamp is the operating system for cohort communities and trusted groups.

Use cases

Start with groups that already have cadence and a clear organizer.

That is where the switch story is strongest and the value is easiest to explain.

Bootcamps and fellowships

Defined lifecycle, recurring sessions, high coordination pain, and immediate value from structure.

Campus groups

Dense, segmented, trust-sensitive communities that need permissions and moderation.

Accelerators and founder groups

Organizer-led groups that care about visibility, participation, and follow-through.

Partner and member programs

Org-run communities that need governance, repeatable operations, and an owner.

Niche communities

Creator-led or guide-led spaces built around a shared interest, practice, or goal.

Why switch

Keep existing tools where needed, move the operating work into WeKamp.

Member import, templates, segmentation, shareable surfaces, integrations, export paths, and payment support matter because organizers will not replace everything on day one.

Trust

Trust becomes useful only when people can see it, understand it, and act on it.

That learning came from kampd-digest, where structured context, validation, fallback, and operator visibility made trust visible and useful.

The Trust Meter is private by default, updates daily, and reflects consistent, thoughtful, constructive participation. Guides can earn bonus trust. Badges and role flair stay separate.

Show the meaning, not the math.
